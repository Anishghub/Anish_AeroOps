TANK CLEANING

Periodicity: Static: Once in three years
Mobile: Yearly

Purpose

Periodically clean the storage tanks to prevent accumulation of dirt, water and other foreign bodies and deterioration of fuel stored in the tank with requisite maintenance.

Reference

OISD 129, AQCAM Appendix-V

Responsibility

Quality Control In-Charge/ Maintenance In-Charge / QCS

Note

The maintenance in-charge must also arrange to carry out ultrasonic shell thickness survey of shell, roof and bottom plates of static ATF tank (s) once in three years. This can be done when the tank is taken up for cleaning. Pls. refer AOM clause 07.04.05 for min. acceptable limits.

🛠️ Tools & Equipment Required

  • Check from PDN & SAP whether the subject consignment with correct valuation type is meant for the location.

Testing Procedure

    1. The QC-in-charge intimates all personnel at the location through the Station / Shift Log Book to the effect that the tank is due and has been taken up for cleaning. Simultaneously, the Maintenance Incharge is advised to take up the job
    2. Based on the above, the Shift-in-charge ensures that the level of product in that tank is brought down to the minimum possible through floating suction as per operational requirement
    3. Thereafter, the tank is isolated by closing and sealing all valves on the inlet and outlet lines. If required, place spectacle blinds or blanks between the valve flanges.
    4. Display proper caution notices regarding tank cleaning in the surrounding area.
    5. The dead stock left in the tank is removed by the Maintenance department into another receptacle or tank in conjunction with QC Incharge, following QC norms.
    6. An appropriate work permit as per OISD Standard 105, (latest one) is prepared for the tank cleaning and maintenance job.
    7. Once the tank is empty, all manholes are opened for ventilation and de-gassing.
    8. The open manhole covers are covered with wire mesh, caution notices are displayed prominently and area should be cordoned off.
    9. The Engineering Officer ensures that the tank has been de-gassed, following all necessary precaution / use of PPE's by checking that:
    10. The LEL measured on the Explosimeter is 0% (i.e. Gas concentration is Nil.)
    11. A minimum of 19.5 % oxygen concentration measured on the oxygen indicator to ensure that human life can be sustained.
    12. Cleaning crew members should be medically fit to carryout the job. A medical certificate should be obtained to this effect.
    13. Only one member of the cleaning crew is sent at a time, inside the tank for cleaning for a maximum of 15 minutes at a stretch. The other crewmembers remain outside with all necessary PPE's access to the person working inside through the safety rope.
    14. Solid contaminants, if any, found in the tank are collected and the weight is recorded in the Tank Cleaning Certificate
    15. Thereafter, the tank is swabbed with plain water. Detergent should not be used for cleaning purpose. After cleaning, the following checks are carried out:
    16. - - -> Shell plates and bottom plates for scaling and condition of epicoating. If not proper, epicoating is touched up or tank repainted as required. Floating Suction assembly is checked for movement and serviceability. If not satisfactory, the swivel balls and seals are checked for wear and tear and replaced if necessary. Besides this, the following checks to be carried out for lanyard :-  Strands of the lanyard should not be broken.  The joints with floating arms should be properly clamped  Lanyard wire should not be rusted.  Length of lanyard to be checked as specified by manufacturer. The condition of the free vent mesh is checked for wear and tear and cleaned. Check proper working of content gauge. Also check there is no wear and tear on body / dial of content gauge.
    17. After the above process, the tank is jointly inspected by the Maintenance and QC in-charges before being boxed up.
    18. Replace the manhole cover gasket with new one and all other gaskets and seals are checked and replaced if required at the time of boxing up the tank in order to ensure perfect sealing. Work permit is now closed with Maint. And QC incharge by finally signing the work permit.
    19. Thereafter, the tank is filled with adequate quantity of product (i.e. 10% of tank capacity) .a minimum soaking period of 48 hour
    20. Till the product passes Test C, the tank will not be considered serviceable for use. After passing the test, a joint certificate of tank cleaning as per Format Q-2 of QHB is prepared. The Test ‘C’ report is attached to the certificate.

Important Note

The Tank Cleaning operation is considered satisfactory when the storage tank is thoroughly cleaned, free from water, sediments, sludge, rust, scale, and any foreign contamination, and all internal components are found serviceable after inspection. The shell plates, bottom plates, floating suction assembly, lanyard, free vent mesh, content gauge, seals, and gaskets must be in proper working condition without wear, corrosion, leakage, or damage.
The tank shall be considered fit for operational use only after completion of cleaning, maintenance checks, proper sealing, and successful soaking with Aviation Turbine Fuel (ATF) for a minimum period of 48 hours. The tank must successfully pass Test “C” before being declared serviceable.
If any abnormality such as corrosion, damaged coating, malfunctioning floating suction system, broken lanyard strands, leakage, contamination, or failure of Test “C” is observed, the tank shall remain unserviceable until corrective actions are completed and the tank meets all operational and quality requirements.
All cleaning details, inspection observations, maintenance activities, contaminant quantities, and final certification shall be jointly recorded by Maintenance and QC In-Charges in the “Tank Cleaning Certificate” as per QHB Format No. Q-2 along with the attached Test “C” report.
